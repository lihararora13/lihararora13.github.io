Kaiseki, the multi-course seasonal meal at the top of Japanese cooking, is usually built on fish stock, which shuts most vegans out of the country's most celebrated dining. Asai Togei in Shinsaibashi is the exception worth knowing. With at least 24 hours' notice, it serves a vegan kaiseki of eight or so small courses: a recent menu ran from pumpkin soup and konnyaku through yuba, mountain yam and lily-root dumplings to tempura, a noodle soup and green-tea mochi.

Lunch sets run around ¥3,500, ¥5,000 and ¥7,000, far better value at lunch than most kaiseki in the city, with private booths and English-speaking staff. Book by email well ahead, say clearly that you need it fully vegan, and mention any other restrictions at the same time. It closes on Sundays.
