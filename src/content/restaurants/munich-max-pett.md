Max Pett has been Munich's vegan institution since 2010, the first dedicated vegan restaurant in the city and still the one every guide starts with. Named after the 19th-century Bavarian chemist Max von Pettenkofer, it cooks the food Munich is famous for without a scrap of animal product: soya schnitzel, vegan cheese spaetzle, stuffed aubergines, mushroom ragout and Kaiserschmarrn, with a changing seasonal menu that leans on local ingredients. Weekend breakfast is a local ritual, and the room has the warm, old-Munich feel of a proper Gasthaus rather than a health-food café.

It sits a short walk from Sendlinger Tor, which makes it the easiest first stop for anyone arriving in the city. Book ahead for dinner, order the schnitzel if it is your first visit, and save room for dessert. This is the table to take someone who thinks vegan food in Bavaria means a sad side salad.
