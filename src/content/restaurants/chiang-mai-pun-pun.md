Pun Pun is less a restaurant than a Chiang Mai institution. It is the kitchen arm of an organic farm and seed-saving project, cooking what its own fields produce that day, and it sits in the grounds of Wat Suan Dok, a temple of white chedis just west of the moat. You eat under the shade of a sacred Bodhi tree while monks drift past, and the menu changes with the harvest: northern Thai curries, Isaan dishes, soups, salads and wraps, most of it vegan, at 60 to 120 baht a plate.

Go for lunch and go early, it closes at 4pm and often sells out before then, and it is closed on Wednesdays. The food is simple and the setting is the point, one of the most peaceful meals in the city.
