Shigetsu is the meal that explains Kyoto: shojin ryori, the Buddhist temple cuisine the city invented, served inside the garden of Tenryu-ji, a Zen temple founded in 1339. The courses are one soup and five to seven seasonal dishes, rice, sesame tofu, vegetable tempura, pickles and sweets, with no meat, fish, egg, dairy or alliums, eaten in a quiet room looking out over the Sogenchi garden.

Book two to three days ahead, add the 500-yen garden admission, and note it is closed on Thursdays. It is the most peaceful lunch in this guide, and the one that makes the bamboo grove crowds feel a world away.
