Kyoto invented vegan food in Japan, more or less: twelve centuries of Buddhist temple cooking, shojin ryori, turned seasonal vegetables, tofu and sesame into a cuisine worth travelling for, and the modern scene has grown up around it. Most of the best tables cluster in walkable downtown Nakagyo and Higashiyama, between Teramachi-dori, Kawaramachi and Gion, with a couple of pilgrimages out to Arashiyama and north Kyoto.

All eight picks are verified open as of November 2026, and all eight are fully vegan. Start with Mumokuteki for a proper set lunch, AIN SOPH. Journey for pancakes near Nishiki Market, and book UZU well ahead for its art-wrapped ramen course. Then queue early for NIJIYA's seven counter seats, squeeze into Zirael for dumplings in Gion, settle into Itadakizen for macrobiotic comfort, slurp Towzen's soy-milk ramen up north, and finish with a shojin lunch in the garden at Shigetsu. A note on the traps: Cafe Phalam stopped being fully vegan years ago, and Gomacro still uses honey in some dishes, so neither made the cut.
