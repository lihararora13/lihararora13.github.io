Kaohsiung does vegan food the way a port city does everything: in a lot of different registers at once. The best tables cluster in Sanmin, around the Kaohsiung Medical University quarter, where a Kyoto-trained soy milk ramen bar sits near a tiny Japanese okonomiyaki counter; in Xinxing, around Liuhe Night Market and Formosa Boulevard, where a night-market stall, a pay-by-weight buffet and a Chinese-technique noodle house sit within a few streets of each other; in Qianjin, where a devotional hot pot restaurant cooks its medicinal broth; in Gushan's old Hamasen quarter, where a farm-to-table cafe grows its own food; and in Zuoying, one MRT stop from the high-speed rail station, where a plant-based eatery doubles as a cat foster home. Most of it is Taiwanese and Chinese cooking first, with Japanese and Western formats alongside.

All eight picks are verified open as of November 2026, six of them fully vegan and two vegan-friendly. Start with a soy milk ramen at Qiu Dou Ru, a classic okonomiyaki at Hola Vege, sesame paste noodles at Zhēn Hǎo Wèi, pot-sticker dumplings at Shou-Yu, a sesame soy milk ramen at Mei Shu Zhai, the medicinal nourishing hot pot at Miào Shàn, the red sauce pulled mushroom burger at MuYe, and a thick soy-milk ramen at JiuSuo. A note on the traps: Hola Vege has about ten seats and no restroom, with the last dine-in order at 6:00pm; MuYe's hours are flexible and it is worth calling ahead, and Mei Shu Zhai closes irregularly each month, so check its fan page; JiuSuo's entrance is easy to miss and Google Maps has shown a clinic at the address, so look for the restaurant sign on Chongshang Street; Shou-Yu's dishes are not always labelled reliably, so check with staff if a dish is vegan; and Zhēn Hǎo Wèi serves some egg and milk dishes alongside vegan ones, so ask for the vegan dishes.
