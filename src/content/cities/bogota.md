Bogotá sits 2,640 metres up in the Andes, and its vegan scene has the same altitude attitude: bold, proud and unapologetically Colombian. Chapinero Alto is the beating heart, a tight grid of streets between Calle 63 and Calle 72 where fully plant-based kitchens sit shoulder to shoulder, but the good stuff spreads from La Candelaria's colourful old town to the leafy brunch streets of Usaquén and the student blocks of Teusaquillo. The city's best trick is taking the dishes Colombia loves, ajiaco, empanadas, arepas de huevo, bandeja, and rebuilding them without a single animal product, then serving them with tree-tomato juice and a grin.

All eight picks are verified open as of November 2026, and all eight are fully vegan. Start with a steaming ajiaco at María Candela, a pecan ragu at Casa Lėlytė, a giant bowl of oatmeal at Herbívoro, a seitan chicharrón at Naturalmente, a burger at Madreselva, a mushroom ceviche at Mestizo Vegano, a chocolate peanut donut at Frambuesa, and an arepa de huevo at ChapiYork. A note on the traps: Artemisa, Gaia and Naturalia all look tempting in guidebooks but we could not verify them against a second independent source, so they stay off the list for now, and the Usaquén branch of Stankov is temporarily closed.
