Taichung does vegan food the way it does most things: without fuss and without a scene to impress. The best tables cluster in the Central District around the TRA station and the century-old Second Market, where a 1968 home-cooked canteen and a market breakfast buffet sit a few streets from a Zen noodle house in a renovated old building, while the West District adds an oat-milk ramen bar and a department-store dim sum restaurant, Nantun adds a small stir-fry house that cooks without onion or garlic, and the East District adds a big pay-by-weight buffet one block from the station. Most of it is Taiwanese and Chinese cooking first, plant-based by tradition rather than by trend.

All eight picks are verified open as of November 2026, five of them fully vegan and three vegan-friendly. Start with the fried king oyster mushroom business meal at Guan Shi Yin, the signature flat noodles and dumplings at Jiu Liu Su Shi, a soy milk white broth ramen at Vegan Raman, a pay-by-weight plate at A-Hua, the three-cup lion's mane mushroom at Qǐ Ruò Yǎ, the heritage minced-mushroom mixed noodles at Sānjīn, the quinoa eight-treasure rice at Hi-Lai, and a pay-by-weight tray at Pure Gourmet. A note on the traps: the VegesM noodle bar has two branches and the North District one is flagged maybe closed, so use the West District address on this page; Guan Shi Yin and Hi-Lai both serve dairy and egg dishes alongside vegan ones, and Pure Gourmet labels its egg and milk dishes but shares tongs, so ask if you have an allergy; A-Hua is a morning stall that closes early in the afternoon and on Saturdays, Qǐ Ruò Yǎ is closed Monday and Tuesday, and Sānjīn's hours differ between HappyCow and local blogs, so check before you go.
