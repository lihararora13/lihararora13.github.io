Johor Bahru is not a walking city for vegans, it is a driving city, and this guide is spread across it: Taman Johor Jaya in the east, Mount Austin's food hub, Taman Ekoperniagaan near the airport, Bukit Indah and Puteri Harbour in the west, Taman Sentosa near the centre, and one new cafe on the old town's heritage street, a short walk from the Singapore border. That spread is the scene's character. There is no single vegan quarter; instead there are strong single rooms in every suburb, from a food-court stall to a beach bar in a shipping container, and the best days pair one suburb's pick with the area around it.

All eight picks are verified open as of November 2026, three of them fully vegan and five vegan-friendly. Start with the daily dishes at Homely Vege Station, the laksa at Vegan Planet, a smoothie bowl at Caribbean Juice Bar, the chee cheong fun at Shan Vege Dim Sum, the kampung rendang poke tup at Kafe MeeTup, the signature lei cha at PMV, the spicy black sauce stone pot noodles at Noodleface, and the otak-otak at Tan Fulin Xuan. A note on the traps: Monday and Tuesday are the closing days for much of this list, Homely Vege Station closes both, Vegan Planet closes Tuesdays, Noodleface and PMV close Mondays, so plan the week. Two pins are neighbourhood-level, Vegan Planet in Mount Austin and Noodleface at the Akademik Suite complex, because the geocoder could not resolve their unit numbers. And a warning on the closures: Loving Hut, the international vegan chain, was reported closed in June 2024 and is not in this guide despite stale listings that still show it, nomVnom is permanently closed, and Holy Smokes Vegan Cafe in Taman Sutera Utama could not be verified to a street address, so it is held as a reserve rather than published.
